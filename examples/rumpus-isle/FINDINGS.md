# What Rumpus Isle found

Every problem met writing this game from the knowledge package (skills and wiki) and the generated types as a game author would, in the order met. Nothing in the engine was changed; each workaround is named, and the code carries it with a comment naming the finding's number. Engine source was not read: `packages/engine/types/index.d.ts` was read for the API, `packages/engine/src/node/polish.ts` for what the hit-feedback measure counts (#13), and `examples/brasswick` for the form. Ratings: **blocker** (the documented way does not work), **annoyance** (costs real time, or silently wrong), **nit** (small, cosmetic, docs only). Engine 20954c20 (main after wave 13: Lantern Vale's, Hollowbell's, The Kestrel Stair's, Meridian Loop's and Brasswick's findings fixed there are not repeated, and the ones still met are named where they are), 2026-10-22; dev-profile `og` (`target/debug/og`) and addon. The round was begun by the owner on a machine with a renderer and stopped at the day's wrap-up (`docs/phase-1.md`), then finished in a sandbox with no GPU (a Mesa lavapipe ICD only): tier none there (`OG_RENDERER=none`, #7 says why), so every frame of the playtests is unchecked and `references/` is not recorded; the eleven frames were rendered once on lavapipe and read by eye. Main 81659882 (release 0.1.1) was merged at the end; its docs were read again against every finding and none is fixed there. Commands run from the repository root, `og` = `target/debug/og`.

Top five: #9, #7, #10, #14, #3.

1. **`create --premise` picks a 2D flipbook look for a 3D party premise** — nit. `bun packages/create/bin/create.ts <dir> --premise "a local-multiplayer party game for two to four on one screen: round critters on a little island bump each other into the sea in short minigames; bright, bouncy, cartoon" --no-prompt` matched `rubber-hose-animation` (og-taste §12: "inked like early theatrical cartoons", a 2D style of flipbooks and squash) for a 3D profile it rightly chose; the preset's outlines and flat shading have nothing to say to spheres on a diorama. Repro: that command, then `grep extends <dir>/assets/looks/<id>.ogt`. Workaround: `extends "miniature-diorama"` by hand (#1 in `assets/looks/rumpus-isle.ogt`).
2. **No `Font()` field factory** — nit (API gap). og-core §4: "a handle wants a field of its own kind ... `Asset()`, `Texture()`, `Sound()`, `Geometry()`, `Material()` or `Entity()` (each handle type of the API has its factory)"; `og.load.font` answers a `Font` handle, and there is no `Font()` (nor `Skin()`), while `Asset` is "a loaded bundle, scene or glTF asset", so a `Font` is not one without a cast. Repro: `grep -n "function Font(" packages/engine/types/index.d.ts` (nothing). Workaround: the display font's handle for the name tags is an `f64` (#2 in `src/components.ts`, `Game.font`, cast back in `src/feel.ts`).
3. **Nothing in the skills points at `og.Camera.worldToScreen` for text over a world point** — nit (docs). og-ui §3 names screen text and world text and og-feel §7 floating text, but a name tag over a critter (screen text that follows a world point, under the UI) is in neither, and `Camera.worldToScreen` is only in the wiki's `camera.md` and the types, so the round's first draft projected by hand with the camera's pose and fov (the owner's, on the lane; it matched `worldToScreen` within 1e-5 px when found). World text (`og.render.text` without `screen`) is not the answer: it faces +z and rides the tone curve and the depth of field, so a tag reads tinted and soft at the frame's edge (seen on the lane's frames before the stop; the screen-text tags are crisp on the lavapipe frames of #7). Workaround: `cam.worldToScreen` and `screen: true` (#3 in `src/feel.ts`, `Tags`).
4. **A game command may not be named `pause`** — nit (docs). og-game §3 says commands are "snake_case actions"; `pause` is a core action of the channel (og-build §1) and the cook refuses it ("'pause' is a core action; register under another name", as Brasswick's round met), with no list of the names a game may not take in og-game. Workaround: `open_pause` (`src/main.ts`).
5. **The form of a command with two arguments is not written** — nit (docs). og-game §3 shows one argument (`0.5:spawn_wave:3`) and og-build §1's `usage` one (`<count>`); nothing says the arguments are the colon-separated fields after the name, so `records:12 34.5` was the first try (`bestBerries: got null`). Workaround: `records:12:34.5`, with `usage: "<berries>:<seconds>"` (`src/main.ts`, `tests/frames.og.ts`).
6. **Two steps at one time naming one file write `<name>-2.json`, and warn** — nit. og-build §1 says unnamed `get` answers on one tick go to `get-<tick>.json`, `get-<tick>-2.json`; two named steps at the same time with the same name (`0.5:get:Game:met.json` twice, a copy-paste) do the same, and the channel resolved it, but as a WARN ("'met.json' was already written by the 0.5s get step, so this one is 'met-2.json'; give each step its own file name"), which a playtest asserting a clean run (og-build §3) fails on. Workaround: distinct names.
7. **In a sandbox with no GPU but a Mesa lavapipe ICD, a headless `og run`, `og test` and `og polish` render every tick on the CPU: a 16 s playtest is minutes** — annoyance (docs). Nothing says a headless run renders at all; it takes the first adapter wgpu finds, here lavapipe (`/usr/share/vulkan/icd.d/lvp_icd.x86_64.json`), at about a quarter of a second a tick at the default size. Measured, `og run . --headless`: `0.1:quit` 3.2 s, `1.0:quit` 17.4 s, the same with one `screenshot` 25.6 s, the frames playtest's 16 s script 4 min at 1280x720; with `OG_RENDERER=none`, `1.0:quit` is 0.5 s. So the first draft's loop (nine playtests and a polish, every one rendering) was an hour of waiting a round. `OG_RENDERER=none` takes the renderer away and the run is the same but for the frames, which it reports unchecked (`og test: 9 suite(s) passed; 11 frame(s) unchecked`); the `none` tier is documented in the wiki's `render.md` and `docs/ci.md`, not in og-build: §1 says a sandbox with no GPU *is* tier `none` ("`og run --json` says `"tier": "none"`, `og doctor` says the renderer is `none`"), where here `og doctor` says `renderer vulkan-cpu (a 64x64 frame headless in 843 ms)` and nothing says what a headless run does with a software adapter or how to tell it not to; §3 explains the "no renderer, frame unchecked" line without the switch that gets there. It was found by reading the engine's docs/ci.md. Workaround: `OG_RENDERER=none` on every `og` call here, and this round's `references/` not recorded: `metal` is the tier kept (docs/ci.md: vulkan-cpu set aside 2026-10-22, a tier with no directory is judged against metal), so the coordinator records them with `OG_FX_CAPTURE=1 og test examples/rumpus-isle` on the Mac. The frames script was run once on lavapipe here and its eleven frames read by eye (nothing to compare them with); what they showed is in the README.
8. **`get:<entity>.og.Transform` answers `position` as `[x, y, z]`; `set:` and `world.set` take `{x, y, z}` / `x,y,z`** — nit. A playtest reading a dump indexes an array where the code it mirrors reads fields. Workaround: the game's facts carry each seat's `x`, `y`, `z` (`src/main.ts`), and the playtests read those.
9. **`og.CollisionStarted` arrives the tick after the step, when `og.Velocity` already holds the contact's answer: a bump's closing speed read from it is never positive** — annoyance (docs, API gap). og-game §13 says contacts "arrive as events" and og.Velocity is "written for dynamic bodies every tick", and the event carries `point` and `normal` but no relative speed or impulse; a system reading the two bodies' `og.Velocity` on the event's tick sees them already parting (the solver's answer), so `max(0, closing)` was 0 on every bump in the round's first draft, every shove fell to its floor and no bump had hit stop, which `og polish` reported (hit feedback 83) and nothing else did. Repro: on 9f9c05f1, a `console.log` of `closing` in `Bumps` (`src/critters.ts`) and `og run examples/rumpus-isle --headless --commands "0.2:seat:wasd;0.3:seat:arrows;0.4:match;0.5:go;0.8:key_down:KeyA;0.8:key_down:ArrowRight;1.6:key_up:KeyA;1.6:key_up:ArrowRight;2.0:quit"` with `OG_RENDERER=none` (two critters driven head-on: `0.00` at every contact). Workaround: `Critter.vx`/`vz` keep the velocity `Drive` hands the step, and `Bumps` runs before `Drive` so it reads last tick's (9.23 m/s for the head-on above), with the event's `normal` for the line (#9 in `src/components.ts`, `src/critters.ts`, `src/main.ts`). The event could carry the relative velocity at contact, or og-game §13 could say what og.Velocity holds on the event's tick.
10. **A control that takes the focus after a screen transition takes it late: a press in the first 0.7 s is lost** — annoyance. og-ui §4: "What opens takes the focus"; after `title` (Escape from the lobby, an iris) the main menu's Play is built on the first tick of the title but answers Space only about 0.7 s after the transition begins, with no word on when, so a playtest's (or an eager player's) press at 0.6 s does nothing. Repro: in `tests/lobby.og.ts` move the `press("Space", 2.4)` after `press("Escape", 1.6)` to 2.2 (the title's phase is reached by 2.1, `title.json`). Workaround: the press at 2.4 s (#10 in `tests/lobby.og.ts`).
11. **An `f32` field widens in an og.platform payload** — nit. `records.crumble` written from a `f32(2.7)` arrives in `dump_platform` as `2.700000047683716`, so a playtest cannot compare the payload with the fact by `===`; og-platform-sdk §2 does not say the store keeps what JSON made of the float. Workaround: a tolerance (`tests/save.og.ts`).
12. **The iris keeps the previous screen on the surface for half a second** — nit (docs). og-ui §6 says to build the screen the transition answers; it does not say the old one stays until the iris closes (about 0.5 s), so a `dump_ui` right after a phase change lists the old screen's texts (the lobby after `match`, the title after `title`). Workaround: the dumps at +0.5–0.6 s (`tests/flow.og.ts`, `tests/locales.og.ts`).
13. **og polish asks for a floating number on every `hits` fact** — nit (og-build §5). A party game's bumps are hits (the measure needs a fact named so), but a number over each would be noise, and og-feel §7 itself says floating text is for "damage and score numbers"; hit feedback scores 100 on three signals of four, yet its fixes table lists "had no floating text within 8 ticks" for every bump of every playtest (three rows of the report's four), which a reader takes for work owed. The table could leave the fourth signal out where three are whole.
14. **`og test` runs every suite or none** — annoyance. `og test [project] [--json]` is the whole usage: nine playtests are 40 s a run (the first draft's, with the GPU probe of #7, an hour), and a loop on one suite reruns the eight others. og-build §4 names no filter. Workaround: `og run` with the suite's script by hand while iterating, `og test` at the end.

## What worked well

og cook: 40 assets with no warnings, three glTF models from a script (`tools/glb.ts`) with their materials, a 30 s stereo music streamed in chunks, og sfx presets for every sound, the cartoon skin pack copied and recoloured. Dynamic sphere critters with locked rotation about x and z, damping and no gravity drove, dashed, bumped (`og.physics.applyImpulse`, a squash each) and fell into `og.Water` with an `og.WaterSurface` that splashed; a kinematic island sank under a grid of kinematic tiles that cracked and dropped one by one, and three kinematic boxes made the podium's steps ground (an instantiated glTF has no collider, as og-game §13 shows: one is inserted). Six input sources on one world, four pads and two halves of the keyboard, sat down in a lobby where every pad has its own focus ring (`og.ui.setPlayers`) and the keys pick colours with left and right; `og.ui.screenTransition`'s iris between every screen, `og.ui.settingsScreen` every word in French from the tables (spaces before `?` and `!`, accents from the cartoon pack's faces), `og.ui.mainMenu` and `og.ui.pauseMenu` with their Back, count-ups and bursts on the scoreboard. og.platform kept the settings and the isle's records across a relaunch at the revision read, with a conflict merged by the best of each; a match by the keys alone from the title through two rounds ended on one hash in two processes; `og.time.hitStop` and `og.time.setScale` froze the critters and not the UI. The playtests: nine suites, 216 checks, 40 s together with no renderer.
